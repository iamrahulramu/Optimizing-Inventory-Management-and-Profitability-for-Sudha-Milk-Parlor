# Optimizing Inventory Management and Profitability for Sudha Milk Parlor
This project focuses on improving the inventory management and profitability of Sudha Milk Parlor, a local dairy shop in Bihar. The shop faced challenges such as inefficient utilization of limited storage space and product wastage due to the short shelf-life of dairy items. By using the sales and purchase data, detailed analyses were conducted to identify fast-moving and slow-moving products, calculate product-wise profitability, and recommend actionable inventory optimization strategies.

## Project Overview 
Sudha Milk Parlor, located in Hajipur, Bihar, has been operating for over 19 years. The shop sells three different milk products to the customers, namely Sudha Gold Milk, Shakti Milk and Cow Milk. Each of these products is available in two different quantity variants: 500mL and 1L. These milk products differ in terms of the fat content they possess, with Sudha Gold Milk having the highest fat percentage (6%), followed by Shakti Milk (4.5%) and Cow Milk having the least amount of fat content (3%). 

Despite consistent customer inflow, its profitability had plateaued due to inefficient utilization of inventory space and product wastage. Hence, this project aimed to apply data-driven strategies to optimize the shop’s inventory management and enhance its overall profitability. The analysis was carried out on three months of sales and purchase data (from December 2024 to February 2025) to uncover operational inefficiencies and propose actionable solutions. 

The key focus areas included:
  - Identifying best-selling and slow-moving milk products.
  - Evaluating sales conversion rates and profitability of each product.
  - Recommending optimal purchase quantities of individual products to minimize wastage.
  - Proposing strategies like dynamic pricing for near-expiry products.

## Implementation Steps
The following steps outline the systematic approach adopted to implement the project, analyze the collected data, and derive meaningful insights into improving Sudha Milk Parlor’s inventory management and profitability:

### Data Collection
- Daily sales records and monthly purchase logs were collected directly from the shop owner in handwritten form.
- The collected data covered a three-month period (December 2024 - February 2025) and included:
  - Quantities of each milk product sold per day.
  - Daily revenue generated by each product and overall shop revenue.
  - Monthly procurement quantities and purchase costs of each product.
- These records formed the foundation for evaluating product-wise performance, sales conversion rates, and profitability.

### Data Preparation and Cleaning
- The handwritten data logs were digitized using Microsoft Excel and converted into structured ``.xlsx`` files for further analysis.
- During the digitization process, certain inconsistencies were identified:
  - Product naming variations (e.g., “SM1” vs. “Shakti 1L”).
  - Ambiguous handwritten numerical entries (e.g., confusion between “3” and “8”).
- All such inconsistencies were verified directly with the shop owner to ensure accuracy and data integrity.
- Once finalized, the cleaned dataset was used as the input for Python-based exploratory and analytical processes.

### Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)
- Conducted descriptive statistical analyses to understand product performance and revenue trends.
- Generated visualizations to illustrate daily revenue fluctuations, product-wise revenue shares, and sales conversion rates of individual products.
- Derived key performance metrics including:
  - Revenue contribution (%) of each product to total revenue.
  - Sales conversion rate (%) of individual products, comparing quantities sold to quantities purchased.
  - Profit margins (₹) of individual products by accounting for purchase cost and sales revenue.
  - Optimal purchase quantity for each product, derived from average daily sales, restocking frequency (every 5 days), and conversion rates.
- These analyses highlighted both operational strengths (fast-selling products) and inefficiencies (slow-moving items occupying inventory space).

### Derivation of Insights
- Identified fast-moving products and slow-moving products sold in the shop.
- Quantified the mismatch between actual and optimal purchase quantities to identify overstocking or understocking.
- Assessed profitability and wastage patterns, helping to establish a clear link between sales performance and procurement strategy.
- Developed data-driven recommendations to optimize purchasing decisions and resource allocation within the limited inventory space.

## Results
Based on the analyses performed on the collected data, the following results were obtained:

- **Daily Revenue Trend** <br>
The visualization provided a clear understanding of revenue stability, identifying high-demand periods (like the festive season in December–January) and lower-performing periods in February.

- **Product-Wise Revenue Contribution**   
  This analysis revealed that three products - Cow Milk (1L), Sudha Gold Milk (500mL), and Shakti Milk (1L) - collectively accounted for over 80% of the total revenue. Additionally, the 1L variants showed higher profitability than the 500mL variants.

- **Sales Conversion Rate** <br>
  The visualization highlighted that products such as Shakti Milk (1L) and Cow Milk (1L) had conversion rates above 99.8%, marking them as fast-moving, while 500mL variants showed slower movement and potential overstocking.

- **Profit Margin** <br>
  The analysis revealed that Sudha Gold Milk (500mL) was the most profitable product, followed closely by Cow Milk (1L). Conversely, Sudha Gold Milk (1L) incurred a net loss, making it a key candidate for purchase quantity reduction in future restocking cycles.

- **Optimal Purchase Quantity**  
  The visualization revealed that high-performing products (like Shakti Milk 1L and Cow Milk 1L) were under-purchased, whereas slow-moving ones (like Sudha Gold 1L) were over-purchased.

## Recommendations
Based on the insights derived from different analyses, the following recommendations were proposed to the shop owner to improve the overall efficiency of the shop's operations:

- **Optimal Purchase Quantity**
  - Developed a formula to determine the optimal restocking level for each product based on actual sales trends.
  - This formula helps maintain sufficient stock of high-demand products while minimizing overstocking of slow-moving items.

- **Dynamic Pricing Mechanism**  
  - Proposed a tiered discounting strategy for near-expiry products to reduce wastage and recover potential losses:
    - Products expiring in 2 days → 25% discount
    - Products expiring in 1 day → 50% discount
    - Products expiring on the same day → 75% discount
  - This strategy ensures improved turnover, reduced product wastage, and better utilization of inventory space.

- **Inventory Prioritization**
  - Recommended focusing on 1L variants of milk products, as they consistently outperformed the 500mL variants in terms of both sales volume and profit margins.
